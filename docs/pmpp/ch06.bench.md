# Matmul Kernel Benchmark

On an NVIDIA A100X locked at 1215 MHz, `matmul.coarse` with tile width 32 is
the fastest tested kernel. The benchmark uses square FP32 matrices with
`M = N = K = width`. Widths 4096 and 8192 show the same large-problem
behavior.

## Configurations

| Kernel                    | Outputs per thread | Block shape | Tile widths | Coarse factors |
| ------------------------- | -----------------: | ----------- | ----------: | -------------: |
| [`matmul.cell`][cell]     |                  1 | `16 x 16`   |           — |              — |
| [`matmul.tile`][tile]     |                  1 | `T x T`     |      16, 32 |              — |
| [`matmul.coarse`][coarse] |                `F` | `T x T`     |      16, 32 |       4, 8, 16 |

The tracked tile and coarse kernels use `T = 16`; coarse uses `F = 4`.
Other results use temporary builds with only these constants changed. `T = 64`
cannot launch because a `64 x 64` block contains 4096 threads; the device limit
is 1024.

Factor 4 is fastest in every coarse comparison. Factor 8 remains slightly
faster than the tile kernel only at tile width 16; factor 16 is slower than the
tile kernel at both widths.

## Memory Traffic

Nsight Compute measured one launch of each kernel with cache control set to
`all`. DRAM total is the hardware `dram__bytes_read.sum` plus
`dram__bytes_write.sum`. L2 and global-load measurements are 32-byte sector
counts; shared-memory measurements are LSU wavefront counts, not transferred
bytes. The reductions compare factor-4 coarse with tile at the same width and
tile width.

| Width | Tile |  Tile DRAM | Coarse DRAM | DRAM reduction | L2 read reduction | Global-load reduction | Shared-store reduction |
| ----: | ---: | ---------: | ----------: | -------------: | ----------------: | --------------------: | ---------------------: |
|  4096 |   16 |  17.259 GB |   10.068 GB |         41.66% |            34.34% |                37.99% |                 38.47% |
|  4096 |   32 |   8.729 GB |    2.638 GB |         69.78% |            39.62% |                37.50% |                 38.66% |
|  8192 |   16 | 138.111 GB |  137.221 GB |          0.64% |            30.06% |                38.18% |                 38.45% |
|  8192 |   32 |  69.631 GB |   54.240 GB |         22.11% |            32.10% |                37.50% |                 38.45% |

Factor 4 consistently removes about 37.5% of global-load sectors: it reuses an
A tile across four output tiles, while B loads and C stores remain. It also
removes about 38.5% of shared stores. Shared loads remain nearly constant
because the implementation repeats the shared-memory reduction for every
output.

DRAM traffic does not follow the global-load count. Cache hits vary with matrix
and tile shape: at 8192 with tile width 16, coarse removes 38.18% of global-load
sectors and 30.06% of L2 read sectors, but only 0.64% of DRAM bytes. The tile
and coarse L2 read-hit rates are 39.81% and 14.46%, respectively. Source-level
loads and logical-traffic models therefore cannot predict DRAM traffic.

Factors 8 and 16 reduce measured DRAM traffic further, but lose performance
because they lower occupancy and retain serial shared-memory reductions and
barriers. The raw reports and the full factor comparison are kept in
`.ai/bench/ncu/memory-analysis.md`.

The extra accumulators raise register use. At tile width 16, factors 4, 8, and
16 permit 64, 48, and 32 resident warps/SM. At tile width 32, factor 8 crosses
the register limit for two 1024-thread blocks and cuts residency from 64 to 32
warps/SM. The kernel also finishes one output's reduction before starting the
next, so the extra accumulators do not expose parallel FFMA chains. The lower
occupancy outweighs the smaller traffic model.

The tile kernel remains below a 2x cell speedup at `T = 16` because logical
load reduction does not equal instruction reduction. Shared-memory loads,
address arithmetic, loop control, and barriers replace part of the global-load
work, while caches and coalescing already help the cell kernel.
